A toy rotation keeps a small set of toys in reach and stores the rest out of sight. You swap a few when interest drops, and you leave a comfort favorite out. The aim is a calmer floor, and a better chance your child stays with one toy. It is not a curriculum, and it is not a reason to buy more.

How to rotate toys is the same job at any age. What changes is the number out, the safety checks, and the storage. This page is the general system. A Montessori shelf, with trays and a slow demonstration, is a different setup, in <Link href="/blog/montessori-toys">Montessori toys</Link>.

**Quick answer:** Sort what you own. Remove what is broken or recalled. For a toddler, about 6 to 10 toys plus a few books is a starting suggestion, not a research number. Store the rest where a child under 3 cannot reach small parts. Swap 2 or 3 toys when interest drops, or about once a week or once every two weeks. Then play together.

## Why rotate toys

[Dauch and colleagues](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29190457/) tested whether fewer toys changed toddler play (*Infant Behavior and Development*, 2018). Thirty-six children, ages 18 to 30 months, visited a University of Toledo lab twice: 4 toys on one visit, 16 on the other. A researcher supervised. A caregiver came in only if being apart was too hard. The team scored 15 minutes. Sessions averaged about 20 minutes and stopped by 30.

With 4 toys, children switched less, stayed longer with one toy, and used toys in more ways. Averages were about 10 play incidents versus about 20, about 117 seconds per incident versus about 56, and about 11 ways of playing versus about 7. Ways meant actions such as dumping or pretending. Times varied widely, so many children were not near the average.

The [University of Toledo release](https://news.utoledo.edu/index.php/12_18_2017/fewer-toys-lead-to-richer-play-experiences-ut-researchers-find) quotes lead investigator Alexia Metz: with fewer toys they “played twice as long.” Her home advice was to hold some gifts back and swap them in later. The sample was small and mostly girls. The paper limits the finding to typically developing toddlers like those enrolled. It was a short lab study. It did not test a weekly or fortnightly rotation. The authors only suggest storing most toys and rotating a small set.

Which object is out matters less than whether you are in the play. Aleeya Healey and Alan Mendelsohn, in the American Academy of Pediatrics report [Selecting Appropriate Toys for Young Children in the Digital Era](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30509931/) (*Pediatrics*, 2019), say evidence that toys support development comes mainly from play with a caregiver. The toys most likely to help are ones you enjoy using together, with talk and a little help. A good toy does not have to be expensive. One clear job, when focus is the goal, is in <Link href="/blog/toys-improve-focus-concentration">toys for concentration</Link>.

## Five steps to set a rotation up

1. **Gather and sort.** Separate what is still loved, ignored, and damaged. If a skill has no material, the age guides name a type: <Link href="/blog/educational-toys-for-1-year-olds">1-year-olds</Link>, <Link href="/blog/educational-toys-for-2-year-olds">2-year-olds</Link>, and <Link href="/blog/educational-toys-for-3-year-olds">3-year-olds</Link>. The wider test is in <Link href="/blog/educational-toys-child-development">choosing educational toys</Link>.

2. **Remove what is unsafe.** [NAEYC](https://www.naeyc.org/resources/topics/play/toys) says wear can turn a safe toy into a hazard, and it points families to recalls. Search [CPSC recalls](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls). Retire a recalled, splintered, cracked, or loose-eyed toy, and any battery door that opens by hand. Ages about 1 to 3 are in <Link href="/blog/safe-toys-for-toddlers">safe toys for toddlers</Link>. The first year is in <Link href="/blog/safe-toys-infants">safe toys for infants</Link>.

3. **Group by play type.** Separate blocks, books, pretend props, and movement toys. One of each kind can stay out. Piece size for a puzzle is in <Link href="/blog/wooden-puzzles-for-toddlers">wooden puzzles for toddlers</Link>.

4. **Leave a small set out.** About 6 to 10 toys, plus a few books, is a starting suggestion for a toddler. It is not the lab’s 4-versus-16 comparison, and it is not an official count. Books can stay for <Link href="/blog/reading-aloud-language-development">reading aloud</Link>. If they only use two toys, leave two.

5. **Store the rest and swap 2 or 3.** Hide the stored pile. Small parts stay where a child under 3 cannot reach them. NAEYC says under-3 toys should not have pieces that could lodge in the throat. Keep the comfort toy out. A day with no new toy, at about age 2, can use <Link href="/blog/montessori-activities-2-year-olds">Montessori-inspired activities for 2-year-olds</Link>.

## What to leave out, by category

Examples are types you may already own. The last column is a starting amount for a toddler, not a Toledo finding.

| Category | Examples | How many to leave out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Building | Unit blocks or a few stacking cups | One small set, not the whole bin |
| Puzzles and fitting | One wooden puzzle with large pieces | One |
| Pretend | A doll or soft animal, a pot, a hat | One or two props |
| Movement | A soft ball or a push toy | One |
| Books | Board books | A few; these can stay out |
| Comfort | The stuffed toy they ask for | Keep that one out |
| Art or sound | A crayon and paper, or a pot and spoon | One setup, with you nearby |

A second puzzle waits until the first is ignored. Wipe blocks before they return, using <Link href="/blog/clean-wooden-toys">how to clean wooden toys</Link>.

## How often to rotate

There is no researched schedule. Once a week and once every two weeks are both fine starts. Keep one plan for a month, then follow the child.

Swap sooner if toys are dumped in the first minute. Wait if a puzzle or a pretend scene is still being repeated. For a toy rotation for toddlers, put two new pieces out and sit down with them.

## By age

**Under 1.** Leave a few safe objects you stay beside: something to hold and mouth, something to look at, and a board book. NAEYC’s infant lists start there. Use the infant safety checks before anything goes on the floor.

**About 1 to 2.** They carry, dump, fill, and cruise. Keep pieces large: a cup, a book, a ball, and one push toy can be most of what is out. How many toys should a toddler have out is still a handful. If they mouth toys near age 2, stay with large pieces.

**About 2 to 3.** NAEYC lists wood puzzles of about 4 to 12 pieces, blocks, and simple dress-up, and says under-3 toys should not have small parts. Follow the size rule first. One puzzle, one pretend prop, and a small set of blocks are enough.

**About 3 to 5.** NAEYC says preschoolers usually attend longer than toddlers and can begin to share. Leave a slightly larger set once mouthing has stopped, and still store the overflow. Skill detail for age 3 is in the guide linked above.

## Storage in a small home

Use one low place for toys in play and one place the child cannot browse. An open shelf or a low basket holds the out set. A lidded opaque bin, or a closet shelf, holds the rest, so pictures are not calling from across the room. Keep that bin off the play floor. A chest a child can climb into, or a lid that can fall, is covered in the storage section of the toddler safety guide.

One basket per category speeds the swap. In one room, use a wardrobe or a high shelf, not a gap a child can crawl into. Small parts go up high. If the living room is the only storage, own less.

## When rotation is not the answer

A rotation tidies a crowded room. It does not answer a worry about development.

The CDC’s [2-year milestones](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/2-years.html) list what most children, 75% or more, do by that age, including two words together, kicking a ball, and playing with more than one toy at a time, such as putting toy food on a toy plate. The lists are not a screening test. If a milestone is missing, a skill has been lost, or something else concerns you, the CDC says not to wait. Talk with your child’s doctor and ask about developmental screening.

Leave favorites out during a move, an illness, or a hard week. Healey and Mendelsohn place the useful evidence in play with a caregiver, so sit with your child before you blame the shelf.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a toy rotation?

A few toys stay out and the rest are stored out of sight. You swap some when interest drops, or about weekly or every two weeks, and a comfort toy stays available.

### How many toys should a toddler have out?

There is no official number. In the Dauch study, children played longer with each toy, and in more ways, with 4 toys than with 16. About 6 to 10 toys plus books is only a home starting point. If they use three, leave three.

### How often should I rotate toys?

No study cited here tested a schedule. Start weekly or every two weeks, wait while a toy is still in heavy use, and swap 2 or 3 items rather than the whole set.

### How do I rotate toys if my toddler still mouths pieces?

Keep loose pieces too large to swallow, and store an older child’s small parts out of reach. The toddler safety guide is the checklist. Rotating does not make a small part safe.

### Do I have to hide toys they still love?

No. Leave the comfort object and anything they are still repeating. Store the overflow and the toys they have dropped.

### Will fewer toys help concentration?

The lab found longer, more varied play with fewer toys in one short session. That is not a diagnosis or a school result. If play, speech, or attention worries you, use the CDC page and talk with your child’s doctor.

## What to try first

Choose about 6 to 10 toddler toys, or fewer if that already looks like a lot, plus the books you read. Put the rest in one opaque bin and leave the comfort toy out. Play with what remains. In a week, swap 2 or 3 ignored toys. If you change only one thing, sit on the floor with the toys already out.
