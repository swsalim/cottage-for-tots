A scribble is the drawing. At 18 months, most children scribble, and that mark is the milestone. You do not need a house, a sun, or a person. This page is what those marks are doing, and what to say when you see one. The household setups, for about ages 3 to 5, are in <Link href="/blog/art-activities-preschoolers-household-items">art activities for preschoolers</Link>.

## What a scribble is doing

The crayon moves and a line appears. That is cause and effect they can see. The hand is also practicing a hold. How that hold sits next to walking, stacking, and buttons is in <Link href="/blog/gross-fine-motor-skills#development-of-fine-motor-skills">how fine motor skills develop</Link>.

You can name what is on the page. You cannot read a feeling off it. A dark scribble is not proof a child is angry, and a bright one is not proof they are fine. Art is not therapy, and it is not a test of emotion.

[NAEYC’s 2014 guide to process-focused art](https://www.naeyc.org/resources/pubs/tyc/feb2014/process-art-experiences) describes the useful version: no sample to copy, and no single right result. A comment such as “Look at all the yellow dots” tells the child you saw the work. A later family note, [Encouraging Process Art at Home](https://www.naeyc.org/resources/pubs/tyc/winter2023/art-at-home) (Loretta Fernando-Smith, winter 2023), uses one question if you need one: “What is your plan?” If they have not named the picture, “What is it?” is a quiz. Skip it.

## What changes with age

These are things about 75 percent of children do by the age listed. They are not a grade for today’s page. The CDC explains that limit on its [key points page](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/key-points.html).

| Age | What most children do | What you ask for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 18 months | Scribble | One mark. No picture. |
| 30 months | Crayons, and a straight line to copy if they want | The line, once. Still no house. |
| 3 years | Draw a circle after you show one | Show the circle. A scribble still counts. |
| 4 years | Hold a crayon between fingers and thumb. Draw a person with three or more body parts | Leave a fist grip alone. Do not demand the person. |
| 5 years | Write some letters in their name | One letter they already use, not a worksheet. |

Sources: CDC checklists for [18 months](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/18-months.html), [30 months](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/30-months.html) (reviewed 16 May 2026; the page also suggests crayons and a straight line to copy), [3 years](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/3-years.html), [4 years](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/4-years.html), and [5 years](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/5-years.html) (reviewed 16 May 2026). The 5-year writing line is also the start of print games in <Link href="/blog/reading-readiness-activities-preschoolers">reading readiness activities</Link>.

### 18 months to about 2

Scribbling is the whole job. Tape the paper down so the other hand is free. Make one mark and hand over a short, fat crayon. If they cover the page in a few seconds, that session is finished.

A fist hold is expected. The shift toward fingers and thumb shows up for most children around age 4, not as a drill at age 2. Forcing a pencil grip usually ends the scribbling. The toddler version of this, including when to stop, is in <Link href="/blog/fine-motor-activities-toddlers">fine motor activities for toddlers</Link>.

### About 3

Most children can draw a circle when you show them how. Show it once. If they scribble over your circle, that is still their mark. Do not erase yours so the page looks like a lesson.

A coloring book has already chosen the picture. Blank paper has not. The 2014 NAEYC list includes drawing with crayons and no directions.

### About 4 and 5

A person with three or more body parts is a 4-year milestone for most children, after a long run of marks that are not pictures. If they are still scribbling, ask for another scribble. The person can wait.

Around age 5, most children write some letters in their name. That is print, not a requirement to stop scribbling. One letter they offer is enough. Sound and letter games that use those marks, without a worksheet, are in the reading-readiness guide.

If they name the scribble (“dog,” “mama,” “soup”), use their word once. That naming is the start of a story, which is a different game, in <Link href="/blog/pretend-play-social-skills">pretend play ideas for 3-year-olds</Link>.

## What to put down

One chunky crayon and one sheet taped to the table. Stop while they are still willing.

Crayons can break into bits. Books and crayons are exempt from the small-parts ban because they cannot be made so they never break, and that exemption is not a promise a bitten crayon is safe. Stay beside a child who chews them. If they still mouth toys, the rest of the floor follows the <Link href="/blog/safe-toys-for-toddlers">toddler safety checklist</Link>.

Skip permanent markers, tiny broken crayon ends left in reach, beads, and buttons. Paint, torn paper, and a box to draw on come later, in the preschool art guide. Do not use raw-flour dough as the art material.

## What to skip saying

- “Draw a house.”
- “Hold it the right way” at age 2.
- “What is it?” before they have named anything.
- “Mine looks like this,” with a sample they are meant to copy.
- A reading of their feelings from the colors.

Save the page they made if they want it saved. The point was the mark, not a display.

## Frequently asked questions

### When do children start scribbling?

Most children scribble by 18 months, on the CDC checklist. Some start earlier and some later. The milestone is the mark, not a picture you can name.

### Should I teach a 3-year-old to draw a circle?

Show one circle if they are watching. If they scribble instead, leave the scribble. The CDC line is “when shown,” for most children, not a daily lesson.

### Does scribbling mean they are ready to write?

Writing some letters in their name is a 5-year milestone for most children. Scribbling at 2 is not late writing, and it is not a problem. Letters can wait until they are already making marks on purpose.

### What if my 4-year-old only scribbles?

Keep offering blank paper and one crayon. A person with three body parts is what most children do by 4, not a picture you should demand this week. If you are worried about the hand, the eyes, or that they have stopped using one arm, talk with their doctor. A scribble by itself is not that worry.

### Are coloring books better than blank paper?

Blank paper leaves the mark up to them. A coloring book has already drawn the picture. Use blank paper for this stage. A coloring page can be a rainy-day extra once they are already scribbling on their own.

## What to try first

Tape one sheet to the table. Put down one chunky crayon. Make a single mark and stop. If they cover the page, say what you see, such as “Look at all the lines,” and end while they are still willing.
