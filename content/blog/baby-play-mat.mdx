A baby play mat is a floor surface for awake play in the first year. An activity gym, also called a play gym, is that surface plus an arch of toys to look at, bat, or kick. A tummy time pillow is a different object: a cushion that props the baby. None of these is a bed, and none of them is required for the next milestone.

This page is birth to about 12 months. The five-point check for any infant toy, including pieces that come off a mat, is in <Link href="/blog/safe-toys-infants">safe toys for infants</Link>. How tummy time sits next to later skills, such as sitting, pulling up, and a thumb-and-finger grasp, is in <Link href="/blog/gross-fine-motor-skills">gross versus fine motor skills</Link>. From the first birthday, movement toys are in <Link href="/blog/gross-motor-toys-toddlers">gross motor toys for toddlers</Link>, and toys sorted by skill are in <Link href="/blog/educational-toys-for-1-year-olds">educational toys for 1-year-olds</Link>.

**Quick answer:** Use the floor while your baby is awake and you are beside them. The CDC’s 4-month tips use a blanket with safe toys. Add a mat or a gym only after the safety checks below. The American Academy of Pediatrics says to begin tummy time the day your baby comes home from the hospital: 2 to 3 short sessions a day, about 3 to 5 minutes each, working up to 15 to 30 minutes total a day by 7 weeks. That guidance is on [HealthyChildren.org](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/baby/sleep/Pages/Back-to-Sleep-Tummy-to-Play.aspx), updated 8 September 2026. The World Health Organization’s [2019 guidelines](https://www.who.int/news/item/24-04-2019-to-grow-up-healthy-children-need-to-sit-less-and-play-more) say infants who are not yet mobile should get at least 30 minutes of tummy time, spread through the day while awake, as part of floor play. More floor play is better. A mat does not replace you.

## Play mat, activity gym, or tummy time pillow

The three names get used for different objects. Choosing starts with which object you mean.

A **play mat** or **tummy time mat** is a pad on the floor. Your baby lies on it, on the tummy or the back, and you stay there. The CDC’s milestone pages for [4 months](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/4-months.html) and [6 months](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/6-months.html) both say that playing on the floor or a play mat every day helps a baby move, learn, and explore. Those pages were reviewed 16 May 2026.

An **activity gym** or **play gym** adds a frame and hanging toys. HealthyChildren.org’s [baby activity](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/growing-healthy/Pages/baby-activity.aspx) page describes a play mat with toys suspended above the baby as a way to kick and reach, and as a way to learn that an action makes something happen. The same page says a swing or a bouncy seat does not count as active play.

A **tummy time pillow** props the chest or the body. The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission, in the [Federal Register final rule](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/11/04/2024-25181/safety-standard-for-infant-support-cushions) for infant support cushions, notes that the AAP and the National Institute of Child Health and Human Development endorse tummy time and do not specifically recommend tummy time pillows for it. The AAP’s own tummy-time page tells you to use your face, your chest, or a toy placed in reach. It does not tell you to buy a pillow.

| Object | What it is | What it is for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Play mat | A pad on the floor, with no arch | Awake tummy time, back play, and rolling |
| Activity gym | A mat plus a frame and hanging toys | Looking, batting, and kicking while lying down |
| Tummy time pillow | A cushion that props the baby | Not part of the AAP tummy-time description |
| Blanket on the floor | No product | The awake-play example in the CDC 4-month tips |

## Safety checks before anyone lies down

A play mat is a play surface. Sleep has its own rules, and they are stricter.

### Awake on the floor, asleep in a sleep space

Place your baby on the back for every sleep, night and nap, on a firm, flat surface in a crib, bassinet, portable crib, or play yard that meets CPSC standards. The AAP explains that policy on [How to Keep Your Sleeping Baby Safe](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/baby/sleep/Pages/a-parents-guide-to-safe-sleep.aspx), updated 11 September 2026. A firm surface should not indent under the baby. Any surface that inclines more than 10 degrees is not a safe sleep surface. Soft objects and loose bedding stay out of the sleep area. That includes pillows, pillow-like toys, quilts, comforters, non-fitted sheets, blankets, toys, and bumper pads.

The same page says an awake adult should supervise awake tummy time, and that tummy time supports motor development and helps prevent a flat spot on the head. It also says not to use products for sleep that are not specifically marketed as infant sleep products. If your baby falls asleep in a car seat, stroller, swing, carrier, or sling, move them to a firm sleep surface on the back as soon as you can. The same move applies if they fall asleep on a play mat.

The AAP tummy-time page says to limit time in freestanding swings, bouncy chairs, and car seats, because those add pressure to the back of the head. Floor time on the tummy and on the back is the alternative. WHO adds a time boundary for containers: infants should not be restrained for more than 1 hour at a time in a pram, stroller, high chair, or strapped on a caregiver’s back. Screen time is not recommended. The CDC milestone pages say the same about screens for children younger than 2, and they limit screens to video calls with loved ones. How that fits the rest of the day is in <Link href="/blog/balancing-screen-time">balancing screen time</Link>.

### The cushion rule, including play mats

CPSC’s mandatory standard for infant support cushions is [16 CFR part 1243](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-II/subchapter-B/part-1243). The [Federal Register notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/11/04/2024-25181/safety-standard-for-infant-support-cushions) defines an infant support cushion as an infant product filled with or made of resilient material, such as foam, fibrous batting, granular material, gel, liquid, or gas, that is marketed or intended to support an infant’s weight, or any part of an infant, while reclining or lying down. The definition includes removable covers sold with the product. The examples include tummy time pillows, loungers, and pads and mats.

CPSC declined to exempt play mats. Commenters asked for an exemption for play mats already covered by the toy standard, including a mat whose flat pad is no thicker than 1 inch. The Commission said no. Toy standards do not address the suffocation hazard, and a thin pad can still have sides or borders. The rule is effective 5 May 2025. The [CPSC announcement](https://www.cpsc.gov/Newsroom/News-Releases/2025/CPSC-Approves-New-Federal-Safety-Standard-for-Infant-Support-Cushions-to-Prevent-Infant-Deaths-and-Serious-Injuries) of 16 October 2024 says cushions manufactured for sale after the rule takes effect must meet it.

For those products, the rule requires:

- Firmness testing, so the surface is unlikely to conform to a baby’s face and block the airway
- A sidewall angle greater than 90 degrees, so a baby is not trapped where a side wall meets the lying surface
- A maximum incline of 10 degrees, which CPSC says effectively limits side height to slightly less than 2 inches
- No restraint system that implies a baby can be left alone in the product
- A permanent, conspicuous warning on the product
- Any toy attached to, removable from, or sold with the cushion must meet the toy standard, [16 CFR part 1250](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-II/subchapter-B/part-1250)

CPSC is aware of 79 infant deaths and 124 injuries from 2010 through 2022 associated with infant support cushions. Most were in infants younger than 3 months. Most of the deaths happened when the cushion was used in or on a sleep product: an adult bed or mattress, a bassinet, a crib, a play yard, a couch, a futon, or a toddler bed. In the incident set described in the Federal Register notice, 63 of the deaths involved loungers, 4 involved pads or mats, and 1 involved a tummy time pillow.

CPSC’s use advice, from that announcement, applies while you shop and while you play:

- Never use an infant support cushion for sleep or a nap. If your baby falls asleep, move them to a crib, play yard, or bassinet.
- Stay near and watch your baby during use.
- Use it only on the floor. Do not use it on a bed, sofa, or other raised surface, and do not put it in a crib, bassinet, or anywhere a baby sleeps.
- Keep blankets and other soft items out of and away from the product.

A blanket on the floor for a few awake minutes is the example on the CDC 4-month page. It is not a padded product marketed to hold a baby. The AAP’s version of the same session is awake, supervised tummy time on the floor or on your chest. A foam mat sold for a baby to lie on can be an infant support cushion. Read the warning. If the mat was made before the rule, the use list above still applies.

### Arches, strings, and the crib

Do not hang a mobile or an activity gym over a crib, bassinet, or play yard. HealthyChildren.org’s [How to Buy Safe Toys](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/at-home/Pages/How-to-Buy-Safe-Toys.aspx), updated 7 December 2021, says hanging crib toys, including mobiles and crib gyms, must stay out of the baby’s reach and must be removed when the baby first pushes up on hands and knees, or at 5 months, whichever comes first. Those toys can strangle a baby. Soft objects stay out of the crib for the same reason.

On the floor, the arch is still a set of parts over a baby. Take off loose ribbons and strings. The AAP says to do that on stuffed toys to avoid strangulation. In 2017, CPSC [recalled a floor baby gym](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2018/PlanToys-Recalls-Baby-Gyms) because babies could strangle on the side rope crossbars. Choose a frame without ropes a neck can slip into. When your baby can push up on the hands, grab the arch, or roll into a strap, take the arch off and keep the flat mat.

### Pieces that come off

Babies mouth what they can grab. Toys intended for children under 3 that present a choking hazard because of small parts are banned. A small part is anything that fits entirely, without squeezing and in any direction, inside the [small-parts cylinder](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Business-Education/Business-Guidance/Small-Parts-for-Toys-and-Childrens-Products): 1.25 inches across and 2.25 inches deep. That includes a mirror that unscrews, a tag that tears off, and a link that breaks. The rest of that check, including edges and washing, is in the infant toy guide.

Skip a mat or gym with a button battery or a high-powered magnet. HealthyChildren.org lists both, because a loose battery or magnet can be swallowed or pushed into a nose or ear. If a battery door opens with a coin or a fingernail, that product is finished.

## What to use from 0 to 12 months

The CDC [Learn the Signs. Act Early.](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/index.html) checklists describe what most children, 75% or more, do by a given age. Each age page says the materials are not a substitute for a validated screening tool. They are not a shopping list. Match the floor to the baby in front of you. If a milestone is missing, a skill is lost, or something else concerns you, talk with your child’s doctor and ask about developmental screening. If your baby was born prematurely, tell the doctor that too. The checklist pages ask you to.

| Age | What most babies do | Floor setup | What to change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Coming home to 2 months | Lifts the head when on the tummy; looks at a toy for several seconds | Your face or a toy at eye level, for a few minutes | Build from 3–5 minutes toward 15–30 minutes a day by 7 weeks |
| By 4 months | Pushes up on elbows; holds a toy placed in the hand; swings at toys | A blanket or flat mat, plus one toy to bat or kick | Keep sessions short of fussing. A swing does not replace this |
| By 6 months | Rolls tummy to back; pushes up on straight arms; reaches for a toy | Toys just out of reach, to one side, as a reason to roll | Crib gyms are already gone. Take a floor arch off if they can grab it |
| By 9 months | Sits without support; gets to sitting; passes a toy hand to hand | A clear mat, with a toy a little farther away | The arch is in the way once they sit and scoot |
| By 12 months | Pulls up to stand; walks holding furniture | Clear floor, then a push toy they walk behind | Stop using an overhead gym. Use the age-1 shelf |

### Coming home, through 2 months

Start the day you come home. The AAP’s opening dose is 2 to 3 times a day, 3 to 5 minutes at a time, while your baby is awake and you are watching. Work up to 15 to 30 minutes total a day by 7 weeks, as your baby shows they can enjoy it. A useful moment is after a diaper change or after a nap, before they are hungry or already crying.

By 2 months, most babies hold the head up when on the tummy, move both arms and both legs, and open the hands briefly. Most look at a toy for several seconds. Those lines are on the CDC [2-month checklist](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/2-months.html), reviewed 16 May 2026. The CDC tip for this age is to lay your baby on the tummy when awake and put toys at eye level. Do not leave them alone. If they seem sleepy, put them on the back in a safe sleep space: a firm mattress with no blankets, pillows, bumper pads, or toys.

Some babies dislike tummy time at first. The AAP says to put yourself or a toy in reach, and that the position gets easier with practice. Chest-to-chest counts: you lie back, and the baby lies on your chest and lifts the head to see your face. That is still supervised tummy time. It does not require a mat.

### By 4 months

Most babies push up onto the elbows or forearms when on the tummy, hold a toy you place in the hand, swing an arm at a toy, and bring the hands to the mouth. The CDC [4-month tips](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/4-months.html) say to give safe chances to reach, kick, and explore, including time on a blanket with safe toys, and to avoid keeping a baby in a swing, stroller, or bouncy seat for too long. A floor gym earns its place here only as something to bat or kick while you are on the floor too. One toy is enough. The CDC says babies this age learn by talking, playing, and interacting with people, and that screen time is not recommended under 2 except for video calls. A mat that plays through the session takes that turn away from you.

### By 6 months

Most babies roll from tummy to back, push up with straight arms when on the tummy, and reach to grab a toy they want. Most also put things in the mouth to explore them. The CDC [6-month tips](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/6-months.html) say to put your baby on the tummy or the back and place toys just out of reach, so rolling has a reason. The [4-month tips](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/4-months.html) say babies learn by mouthing things that are not sharp, hot, or small enough to choke on. That is the small-parts rule, applied to every link on the gym.

This is the month the crib rule has already arrived for many babies. Pushing up on the hands, or turning 5 months, ends a hanging crib toy. On the floor, use that same moment as the cue to take the arch down if your baby can reach it. Keep the mat if it still passes the cushion checks. The toy moves to the side, not overhead.

### By 9 months

Most babies get to a sitting position, sit without support, move a toy from one hand to the other, and rake food with the fingers. The CDC [9-month tips](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/9-months.html) say to place toys on the ground or on a play mat a little out of reach and to encourage a crawl, a scoot, or a roll. That is an activity idea. It is not a CDC milestone that most babies crawl by 9 months. Celebrate the way your baby actually moves.

The arch is usually in the way once sitting and scooting start. A clear mat, or a clear patch of floor, is the better setup. The CDC also says to put your baby near things they can pull up on safely, and to baby-proof the room: sharp and breakable things out of reach, medicines and cleaners locked away. A light play-gym frame is not that stable pull-up point. Take it down before pulling up begins.

Texture, sound, and water play for the year after this one are in <Link href="/blog/sensory-play-activities-toddlers">sensory play for toddlers</Link>. Those activities start around 12 months and have their own choking notes.

### By 12 months

Most babies pull up to stand, walk while holding furniture, and pick up a small piece of food with the thumb and finger. The CDC [1-year checklist](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/1-year.html), reviewed 16 May 2026, is the source for those lines, and the toy page that matches them is the age-1 guide. An overhead gym is done. What you want now is clear floor and, when they are pulling up, something stable to walk behind. The age-1 page explains why that push toy is not a seat-in baby walker.

A play mat can stay as a floor boundary if it is still firm, flat, and free of small parts. It is no longer the main toy. The wider buying test, from babies through school age, is in the <Link href="/blog/educational-toys-child-development">guide to choosing educational toys</Link>.

## Play mat ideas by type

These are types, not products. No brand, price, or rating belongs in this list. Use it later as a slot for a specific pick, after the safety checks above.

| Type | What the baby does with it | Safety note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flat play mat | Lies on the tummy or back; later rolls and sits | Awake, on the floor, with you there. Not a bed. Read the cushion warning |
| Activity gym | Looks, bats, and kicks at toys on an arch | Never over a crib. No rope crossbars or loose strings. Arch comes off when they can push up or grab it |
| Tummy time pillow | Props the chest | Not in the AAP tummy-time description. Same cushion rules if you already have one |
| Baby-safe mirror on the mat | Looks at a face | Must stay attached. If it comes off and fits in the cylinder, it is done |
| One attached toy | Holds a rattle or cloth book fixed to the mat | Every piece too big for the cylinder. No button battery |
| Blanket on a firm floor | The same tummy time, with no purchase | If they get sleepy, move them to a bare firm crib, bassinet, or play yard |

Chest-to-chest, which the AAP already describes, covers the first weeks without a purchase. Buy a type when you want one washable surface you can leave on the floor. Skip a row when the blanket already does that job.

Washing belongs with the infant toy guide: a cover you can remove and clean is easier to keep in use than a pad with seams full of milk. Check the care label on the specific mat. This page does not add a cleaning recipe.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between a play mat and a play gym?

A play mat is the pad. A play gym, or activity gym, is a pad with an arch and hanging toys for a baby who is lying down and learning to bat and kick. The arch is a short stage. The pad, or a blanket on the floor, is the part that can last until they are sitting and moving.

### How do I choose a tummy time mat?

Choose a firm, flat surface you will use on the floor while you watch. The AAP’s tummy-time instructions work on a blanket. If you buy a foam mat marketed for a baby to lie on, treat it as an infant support cushion: look for the permanent warning, skip thick raised sides, and do not use it for sleep. Attached toys have to be too large to fit in the small-parts cylinder.

### Is a baby play mat safe for naps?

No. Sleep belongs on the back, on a firm, flat crib, bassinet, or play yard that meets CPSC standards, with no soft objects. CPSC says not to use infant support cushions for sleep or naps. If your baby falls asleep on the mat, move them to that sleep space.

### When should I stop using an activity gym?

Remove any hanging toy from the crib when your baby first pushes up on the hands and knees, or at 5 months, whichever comes first. That is the AAP rule, and it is about strangulation. On the floor, take the arch off when your baby can push up, grab the frame, or roll into a strap. By the time they sit and scoot, a clear floor is the better setup. By 12 months, an overhead gym is done.

### Do I need a tummy time pillow?

The AAP describes tummy time on the floor, on your chest, or with a toy in reach. CPSC notes that the AAP and NICHD do not specifically recommend tummy time pillows. Pillows and loungers are inside the infant support cushion rule because a soft cushion can conform to the face, and because a baby can roll off one if it is left on a raised surface.

### What if my baby hates tummy time?

Keep it short, stay where they can see you, and try again later the same day. The AAP says many babies dislike it at first and come to like it. Chest-to-chest, a toy at eye level, and a session after a diaper change are the adjustments the AAP names. Stop if they are crying hard, and do not leave them to “get used to it” alone. If you are worried about head control or a flat spot, ask your baby’s doctor. This page is not a medical check.

### How is this different from the infant safety guide and the 1-year-old page?

The infant guide is the general toy check: materials, age, small parts, edges, and cleaning. The 1-year-old page starts at about 12 months and sorts toys by skill. This page is only the floor surface for the first year: play mat, activity gym, tummy time, and the cushion rules.

## What to try first

Spread a blanket on a firm floor. Lie down so your baby can see your face. Do one awake session of a few minutes. If they get sleepy, move them to a bare, firm crib, bassinet, or play yard on the back.

Add one type from the table only if that session is hard to repeat: a washable flat mat for your knees, or a floor gym with no ropes for a baby who is starting to bat. Leave the pillow row empty. When they are pulling up, switch to the <Link href="/blog/educational-toys-for-1-year-olds">age-1 toy guide</Link> and the <Link href="/blog/gross-fine-motor-skills">motor-skill comparison</Link>.
