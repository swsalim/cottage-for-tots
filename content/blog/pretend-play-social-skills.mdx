At age 3, pretend play is a short scene with one prop and one line from you. A pot, a doll, a hat, or an empty box is enough. This page is six setups you can do with what is already in the house. It is practice in taking a role and noticing another person. It is not a social-skills program, and it is not treatment.

Before age 3, the scene is even shorter. By 18 months most children play with a toy in a simple way, such as pushing a car, and the CDC [18-month tips](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/18-months.html) suggest a spoon so they can pretend to feed a stuffed animal. By 2 years most play with more than one toy at once, such as putting toy food on a toy plate. That earlier shelf is in <Link href="/blog/educational-toys-for-1-year-olds">educational toys for 1-year-olds</Link>. Feeling words for the same doll are in <Link href="/blog/social-emotional-learning">social-emotional toys</Link>.

## What age 3 is ready for

Most 3-year-olds notice other children and join them, and most can calm within about 10 minutes after you leave, such as at drop-off. Those lines are on the CDC [3-year checklist](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/3-years.html), reviewed 16 May 2026. Milestones are what about 75 percent of children do by that age. They are not a test. The CDC explains that on its [key points page](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/key-points.html).

Joining is new. Keep the group to one other child. Playing beside a friend, each with their own pot, still counts. [NAEYC](https://www.naeyc.org/resources/topics/play/toys) groups the toys the same way from toddlerhood into preschool: dolls, puppets, dress-up clothes, and play food. What changes is how long the scene lasts.

By 4, most children pretend to be someone else during play, such as a teacher or a dog, on the CDC [4-year checklist](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/4-years.html). You do not need that full role at 3. If they put the hat on and bark, follow their idea. If they only stir the pot, the stir is the scene.

| Setup | From the house | You say once | What you might see |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kitchen | Pot, spoon, doll | “The baby is hungry.” | Stirring, dumping, or feeding the doll |
| Shop | Two empty boxes, one large block | “I’ll take the box.” | A handoff, or they keep the box |
| Bedtime | Doll, blanket, one hat | “The baby is tired.” | A cover, or the hat on their own head |
| Check on the doll | Doll, blanket | “The baby is sad.” | A pat, then they walk off |
| A road | Four to six blocks, one car | “Where is the car going?” | A road, or a crash |
| One role, around age 4 | Hat and a loose shirt | Follow the role they started | They are the dog, the cook, or neither |

## 1. Feed the doll

**Props:** one pot, one wooden spoon, one doll or soft animal with embroidered features.

**Setup:** Put the doll next to the pot. Sit on the floor.

**You say once:** “The baby is hungry.”

**Then stop.** If they stir, the scene is working. If they dump the spoon and walk off, that session is over. Do not add a second plot.

The spoon handle has to be too big to swallow. If they still put toys in their mouth, every piece stays outside the [small-parts cylinder](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Business-Education/Business-Guidance/Small-Parts-for-Toys-and-Childrens-Products): 1.25 inches across and 2.25 inches deep.

## 2. Hand over a box

**Props:** two empty food boxes, one large block. No real coins.

**Setup:** You are the customer. They stand by the boxes.

**You say once:** “I’ll take the box.” Hold out a hand.

**Then stop.** A handoff is the social move: they noticed you wanted something. If they will not give it, play beside them with the other box. Forcing a share ends the game. Turns with a clearer rule are in <Link href="/blog/cooperative-play-toys-sharing">cooperative play toys</Link>.

Skip tiny play food, real medicine bottles, and anything that fits in the cylinder.

## 3. Put the doll to bed

**Props:** the same doll, one small blanket, one hat. CDC [2-year activity ideas](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/2-years.html) include grown-up clothes such as shoes, hats, and shirts. One of those is a costume. A bin of clothes is a pile to dump.

**You say once:** “The baby is tired.”

**Then stop.** They may cover the doll or wear the hat themselves. Both are pretend.

Skip a long scarf, a cord, and anything that wraps a neck. Skip dress-up with small buttons if they still mouth toys. Eyes and buttons that unscrew go in the trash with the toy.

## 4. Check on the doll

**Props:** the doll and the blanket. No toy medical kit full of small pieces, and no real pills.

**You say once:** “The baby is sad.”

**Then stop.** A pat is the whole checkup. One feeling word is enough. If you want the longer feelings version, it is in the social-emotional guide. Do not quiz them on why the baby is sad.

## 5. Send one car down a road

**Props:** four to six large blocks and one car that stays in one piece.

**You say once:** “Where is the car going?”

**Then stop.** A crash is still play. Picking the blocks up together is the cooperation. A tower with a question about which pile has more is a different game, in <Link href="/blog/preschool-stem-activities-at-home">preschool STEM activities</Link>.

If a younger child is on the floor, the car and the blocks follow the under-3 size rule. That check is in <Link href="/blog/safe-toys-for-toddlers">how to choose safe toys for toddlers</Link>.

## 6. One role, when they start it

Around age 4, most children pretend to be someone else. At 3, wait until they start the role. If they put on the hat and say they are the dog, you can be the person who asks the dog to sit. One request. If they ignore it, you are done.

Do not assign “you are the teacher” while they are still happy stirring the pot. The CDC 4-year line is a description of what many children do, not a script you have to teach this month.

A puppet can take the role when they will not answer a question about the day. Keep it to one sentence from the puppet. Language play with books is in <Link href="/blog/reading-aloud-language-development">reading aloud</Link>.

## What to leave out

- A 40-piece pretend set. One pot and one doll cover this list.
- Toys that speak a full script so the child never has to talk.
- Real coins, real medicine, grapes, nuts, and popcorn used as props.
- Heat, a real stove, and knives. The pot stays cold. The spoon is wood.
- Loose magnets and coin batteries. The CPSC [magnet page](https://www.cpsc.gov/Safety-Education/Safety-Education-Centers/Magnets) describes the injury when magnets are swallowed.
- A second theme in the same session. Kitchen or shop, not both.

## Frequently asked questions

### What pretend play can a 3-year-old actually do?

A short copied scene: feed a doll, hand over a box, cover the doll, push one car. Most children this age also notice other children and join them. A long story with several roles is the 4-year-old pattern, and only when they start it.

### Do I need a play kitchen?

No. A real pot and a wooden spoon are the kitchen. Buy a play set later if you want it to stay on a shelf, after the pieces pass the size check.

### What if they only play alone?

At 3, joining is new. Playing beside one other child, each with a box, is a fair session. Invite once. If they stay with their own pot, leave them there.

### What if they will not share the doll?

Use the second box, or a second spoon, so there are two of the wanted thing. End the session if the grab continues. A shared turn works better once the game has a rule, which is the cooperative-play guide.

### Does pretend play teach social skills?

It gives them a place to practice one handoff, one feeling word, or one role. That is not a guarantee, and it is not a substitute for time with other children or for help from a clinician if you are worried. The CDC lists are a reference, not a diagnosis.

## What to try first

Put the doll next to a pot. Say “The baby is hungry.” Wait. If they stir or feed the doll, you have the setup. The other five can wait until this one is easy.
