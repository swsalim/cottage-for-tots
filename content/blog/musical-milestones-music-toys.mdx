A music toy is worth keeping when your child makes the sound. A pot and a wooden spoon do that before a keyboard does. This page is how to choose by age, plus a few rhythm games you can play with what you already have. It is not a ranking of brands, and a music toy does not make a child smarter.

The same pot already shows up as a hearing game in <Link href="/blog/sensory-play-activities-toddlers">sensory play for toddlers</Link>. Marching to a beat is in <Link href="/blog/indoor-gross-motor-activities-toddlers">indoor gross motor activities</Link>.

## What the toy is for

The job is small and specific.

- They hit, shake, or press, and a sound happens because they did it.
- They stop when the sound stops.
- They hear a rhyme and a steady beat.
- They take one turn, then hand the drum over.

A toy that plays a whole song while the child watches is a speaker. The CDC [1-year tips](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/1-year.html) suggest pots and pans, or a simple drum. By 2 years, most children try switches, knobs, or buttons on a toy. That is the milestone to shop for: their hand starts the sound.

[Help Me Grow MN](https://helpmegrowmn.org/HMG/HelpfulRes/Articles/toddler) uses music and a simple rhythm to steer toddler movement. A song with a rhyme is also a listening game for later reading, which is covered in <Link href="/blog/reading-readiness-activities-preschoolers">reading readiness activities</Link>. Counting games are a different shelf, in <Link href="/blog/board-games-cognitive-skills">educational board games for ages 4 to 6</Link>.

## Where to start, by age

| Age | Start with | The game | Put it back |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| About 12 months | A pot and a wooden spoon, or one simple drum | One tap, then silence | Cymbals, a small mallet, anything filled with rice or beans |
| About 2 years | That drum, or one large button | They make the sound. The toy does not play the song for them | A battery door a coin can open |
| About 3 years | A bar instrument with one large mallet | Loud, then quiet, on purpose | Pieces that come off in your hand |
| About 4 to 6 | One instrument you pass | Music on, music off, bodies still | A kit of tiny bells if a younger child is in the room |

Milestones are what about 75 percent of children do by an age. They are not a test this toy has to pass. The CDC explains that on its [key points page](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/key-points.html). A shelf sorted by skill, including the pot and the drum, is in <Link href="/blog/educational-toys-for-1-year-olds">educational toys for 1-year-olds</Link>.

### About 12 months

One pot. One wooden spoon with a handle too big to swallow. You tap once. You wait. They tap, or they do not. Continuous banging is still cause and effect. End it when the house has had enough, not because they missed a beat.

Skip cymbals and any mallet that fits entirely, without squeezing and in any direction, inside the [small-parts cylinder](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Business-Education/Business-Guidance/Small-Parts-for-Toys-and-Childrens-Products): 1.25 inches across and 2.25 inches deep. Toys intended for children under 3 that have small parts are banned.

### About 2 years

Stay with one sound they can start. A large button that stays on the toy is enough. If the button comes off in your hand, the toy is finished.

If the battery door opens with a coin or a fingernail, put it back. Keep every battery door closed. A coin-shaped battery is not a music piece.

### About 3 years

A few bars and one thick mallet are enough for loud and quiet. Two mallets can wait until one is easy to hold. Marks made while a song plays are a different activity, in <Link href="/blog/art-activities-preschoolers-household-items">art activities for preschoolers</Link>.

Wipe a wooden instrument. Do not soak it. The method is in <Link href="/blog/clean-wooden-toys">how to clean wooden toys</Link>.

### About 4 to 6

The CDC [4-year tips](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/4-years.html) include freeze dance: music on, music off, bodies still. That is the listening game for this age. One instrument passed between two children is the turn-taking version. How to set up the pass is in <Link href="/blog/cooperative-play-toys-sharing">cooperative play toys</Link>. The feelings side of “stop” is in <Link href="/blog/social-emotional-learning">social-emotional toys</Link>.

If a child under 3 is in the room, the small pieces still follow the toddler rule, even if the box says 4+. That check is in <Link href="/blog/safe-toys-for-toddlers">how to choose safe toys for toddlers</Link>.

## Five games that use one sound

### 1. One tap, then silence

Tap the pot once. Put the spoon down. Wait. Offer the spoon. If they bang without a stop, you still stop. Your pause is the pattern.

### 2. Loud, then quiet

Two taps they can hear, then two they can barely hear. Name them: “loud,” “quiet.” Stop after a few pairs. This is the whole lesson in the sensory-play version of the pot.

### 3. Copy two claps

Clap twice. They clap what they can. Do not correct the number. Two claps they offer is a finished turn.

### 4. Stop when the song stops

Play a short song you can turn off. While it plays, they tap or march. When it stops, you freeze. At about age 4, the CDC tip is the full version: music on, music off, bodies still. A younger child can freeze the spoon instead of the whole body. Marching with high knees is in the indoor gross-motor guide.

### 5. Pass the drum

One drum. “My turn.” One tap. “Your turn.” Their tap. The drum goes back in the cupboard when the turn gets grabby. A rhyme during the pass is optional. The sound game that uses the rhyme, without an instrument, is in the reading-readiness guide.

## What to leave out

- A shaker filled with rice, beans, beads, buttons, or dry pasta. If the seam opens, those are choking pieces. A taped bottle, and the fills that can stay in it, are in <Link href="/blog/diy-sensory-bottles-toddlers">DIY sensory bottles</Link>.
- Glass, balloons, and a drum made by stretching a balloon over a tin.
- Loose magnets in a “music set.” The CPSC [magnet page](https://www.cpsc.gov/Safety-Education/Safety-Education-Centers/Magnets) describes the injury when two or more are swallowed.
- A toy you have to shout over. Hold it at arm’s length. If you cannot talk across it in a normal voice, it is too loud for a child’s ear. Put it back.
- A cord that ties the toy to a crib or a bed.
- A second keyboard while the pot is still the game they actually play.

An empty cardboard canister, lid taped on, is a drum. Do not put a filling in it to make it a shaker. The tap is the sound.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do music toys make children smarter?

No. They give a child a sound they can start, stop, and repeat. A rhyme in a song is listening practice. A number path is the game with evidence for counting, and that is the board-game guide, not this one.

### What is a good first music toy for a toddler?

A pot and a wooden spoon, or one sturdy drum with no small pieces. The CDC 1-year tips already point at pots, pans, and a simple drum.

### Are maracas and shakers safe?

Only when they cannot be opened and nothing inside is a choking hazard if they crack. Rice, beans, and beads fail that test. Skip them.

### How loud is too loud?

If you have to raise your voice to talk over the toy while it is an arm’s length from you, put it back. Volume you cannot turn down is a reason to leave it in the shop.

### When is a keyboard a good idea?

After they already start and stop a sound on a drum, the keys are large, and the battery door stays shut. One or two notes they choose is the game. A demo song that plays by itself can wait.

## What to try first

Get a pot and a wooden spoon. Tap once. Wait. Hand them the spoon. If they hit it, you already have the first music toy. Buy a drum later if you want something that stores on a shelf, after you have checked the mallet, the battery door, and the volume.
