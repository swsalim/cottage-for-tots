Reading aloud helps language when you are the voice and the child has a turn. One book, one new word, and one question are enough. A recorded story they only watch is a different activity. This page is the shared reading. Rhymes, first sounds, and letters sit next to it, in <Link href="/blog/reading-readiness-activities-preschoolers">reading readiness activities</Link>.

## What the evidence covers

The What Works Clearinghouse practice guide [Preparing Young Children for School](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/practiceguide/30) (August 2022) gives shared book reading a strong evidence rating, from 17 studies that met its standards without reservations. Those studies are preschool classroom programs. A book on the couch is the same kind of practice in a shorter form. It is not those programs, and it does not mean a child will read early or talk early.

The panel recommends at least one shared reading a day in preschool, with the same book or a different one. At home, rereading a favorite is that plan. If they leave after two pages, the session is over. Calling them back to finish the book ends the reason they were listening.

In a study of 26 parent-infant pairs, infants 10 to 16 months old, play with books came with more adult words, more back-and-forth turns, and more parent responses than play with electronic toys ([Sosa, JAMA Pediatrics, 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26720437/)). The useful borrow is the person, not a button that reads the page. Screens are covered in <Link href="/blog/balancing-screen-time">balancing screen time</Link>.

## One reading

1. Pick a book they can hold. A board book for a baby. A short picture book later.
2. Let them turn it the way the cover faces, or turn it with them. The direction of the print, including books that read right to left, is in the readiness guide. Do not stop the story to teach it.
3. Read. Use your ordinary voice. One character can have a different voice if you want. You do not need a performance.
4. On one page, point at a picture and say the new word inside the sentence. “The fox is in the **snow**.” Then keep going.
5. Ask one question, matched to the age below. Wait. If they do not answer, you point or you say it, and you turn the page.
6. Stop while they would still hear it again tomorrow.

| Age | The book | The one question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| About 12 months | A board book they can mouth and hold | None. You name one picture. |
| About 2 years | The same book again | “Where is the bear?” |
| About 3 years | A short story they know | “What is the fox doing?” |
| About 4 years | A familiar story | “What happens next?” |
| About 5 years | The same story | “What happened, and what happened after that?” |

### About 12 months

The CDC [1-year tips](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/1-year.html) say reading can be talking about pictures, and that babies this age like books they can feel or flaps they can lift. You supply the sentence. They may chew the corner. That is still the session.

If a flap tears off and would fit entirely in the [small-parts cylinder](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Business-Education/Business-Guidance/Small-Parts-for-Toys-and-Childrens-Products), 1.25 inches across and 2.25 inches deep, that book is finished. The rest of the size check is in <Link href="/blog/safe-toys-for-toddlers">how to choose safe toys for toddlers</Link>. How this book fits the 1-year shelf is in <Link href="/blog/educational-toys-for-1-year-olds">educational toys for 1-year-olds</Link>.

### About 2 years

By 2 years, most children point to a picture when you ask “where is the bear?” and say at least two words together, on the CDC [2-year checklist](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/2-years.html). Ask once. If they point, you say the word. If they say “bear sleep,” you can add one word: “The bear is sleeping.” Then return to the story. A vocabulary lesson of five words can wait.

Read the same book again this week. Hearing the word in the same place is how it sticks. A new book every night is optional.

### About 3 years

They can turn the page. That turn is their part. Name one new word with the picture, inside a sentence, and move on. Do not ask them to repeat it unless they want to.

A puppet or a stuffed animal can be the character for one line. Then it goes down. Acting the whole book is a different game, in <Link href="/blog/pretend-play-social-skills">pretend play ideas for 3-year-olds</Link>. The 3-year toy shelf for books is in <Link href="/blog/educational-toys-for-3-year-olds">educational toys for 3-year-olds</Link>.

### About 4 and 5

By 4 years, most children tell what comes next in a well-known story, on the CDC [4-year list](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/4-years.html). Ask “What happens next?” once, before a page they know. Accept their guess. Then read what the book actually says.

Two things that happened, in order, can wait until the story is very familiar, closer to age 5. One retelling is enough. Letters, rhymes, and pointing along a line are the other page. If they poke a letter in the middle of the story, you can name it once and keep reading. The game that stays with that letter is in the readiness guide.

Milestones are what about 75 percent of children do by that age. They are not a test of this book. The CDC explains that on its [key points page](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/key-points.html).

## What to skip

- A quiz after every page.
- Stopping the story to define every new word.
- Asking them to sit through a book they have already left.
- An electronic book that reads itself while you watch.
- Letter magnets bought so the reading will “count.” Loose magnets are a hazard. The CPSC [magnet page](https://www.cpsc.gov/Safety-Education/Safety-Education-Centers/Magnets) describes the injury when two or more are swallowed.

The same book tomorrow is a complete plan. You do not need a new title to make the reading count.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does reading aloud help language?

Shared book reading has a strong evidence rating in the 2022 practice guide, from preschool programs. At home, the part you can copy is one reading a day with a person, a picture, and a turn for the child. It is practice. It is not a promise they will talk or read early.

### How long should we read?

As long as they stay. Two pages can be the whole session for a toddler. A familiar picture book is a full session later. Stop when they close it or leave.

### Should I explain every new word?

One word, pointed at in the picture, inside the sentence you are already reading. Then continue. A second word can be tomorrow’s, in the same book.

### What if they will not sit still?

Read while they stand, or while they turn pages out of order. Name the picture they landed on. Order can wait until they want the story they already know. A child who leaves is done.

### Do I need special books?

No. A board book you already have is the first one. Reread it. Add a longer picture book when they stay for the whole short one. The words can be in the language you speak at home.

## What to try first

Tonight, read a book they already like. Point at one picture and say the word in the sentence. Ask one question, or none if they are still a baby. Stop when they are done, even if the book is not.
